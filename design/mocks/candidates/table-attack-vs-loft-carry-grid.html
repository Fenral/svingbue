Attack angle vs delivered loft — carry grid

Seventy solved ball flights. Two clubs, one question: which dial actually moves the ball, and when does it stop paying?

The read

Loft and attack angle are not two dials. They are one. The engine sets backspin from spin loft — delivered loft minus attack angle — so any two cells sharing a spin loft get the same spin, and once the ball launches high enough to fly efficiently they get the same carry. Across these two grids, all 9 spin-loft groups that hold more than one fully efficient cell land on identical carry, to 0.0 yd. The grid runs in diagonal bands, not in rows or columns.

Driver, 110 mph. Whichever dial you already have enough of, the other stops paying. Hitting −5°, the whole 8–20° loft band spreads 35.0 yd of carry. Hitting +7°, that same band spreads 7.7 yd. It mirrors: at 8° of loft, moving attack angle from −5° to +7° adds 51.4 yd; at 20° of loft it adds 17.2 yd. Read loft as a band, never as a direction: the loft that carries farthest slides from 17° when you hit down 5° to 11° once you hit up, and carry falls away on both sides of it — so at +7° attack the 20° row actually ends 0.6 yd short of the 8° row. The best carry the engine calls plausible is 266.0 yd at 11° loft and +3° attack — not the grid's numeric maximum of 267.8 yd, which needs a backspin the engine itself flags as implausible.

Iron, 90 mph. The bands tilt the other way. Loft moves carry about 2.3× as far as attack angle does — median 36.6 yd across the loft range against 16.2 yd across the attack range. That spread is what makes a set of irons a set; attack angle is the trim, not the lever. Read the iron grid with its caveats loud: 24 of 35 cells are flagged, because every ascending strike is a shot this model was not built to describe.

What to do with it. Read the grid along its diagonals. If you are already delivering plenty of loft, buying attack angle returns little. If you deliver little loft, attack angle is the largest single lever on this page — and the numbers in the shallow-loft corner get less trustworthy the further up you swing.

Caveat marks

1
Clamped. The engine hit one of its own ceilings — spin at 9000 rpm, smash factor, or landing angle — so the true value lies beyond the number shown.
2
Implausible strike. The input combination is solvable but not a shot a golfer produces: driver backspin under 1500 rpm, or an iron struck on the way up.
3
Unreliable. The closed-form carry and the RK4 integrator disagree materially. Do not use this number.

Provenance

Every value on this page is re-derived engine output, not transcribed. 70 solveFlight() calls into impact-flight.js, one per cell, as solveFlight({ dynamicLoft: row, attackAngle: col, clubSpeed: preset, clubPath: 0, faceAngle: 0 }). Generated 2026-07-28T06:18:19.597Z. Carry and total are tagged ESTIMATE by the engine; backspin and spin loft are CALCULATED. Both clubs are solved with club path and face angle at zero, so this page describes distance only — it says nothing about curvature or dispersion.
