Face control

The cost of one degree

How far offline a single degree of open face puts the ball. The same mistake is a different mistake in every club in the bag.

One degree of open face costs 11.1 yards with a driver and 2.5 yards with a pitching wedge — the same mistake, 4.4× the punishment. In between sit the 5-iron at 5.7 yd and the 7-iron at 4.5 yd. The longer the club, the more a degree costs.

Inside any one club the cost is a straight line: four degrees costs between 3.90× and 4.02× what one degree costs, in every row here. There is no forgiving first degree and no cliff at the fourth — so you can multiply. Carry barely moves at all; face error is a direction problem.

Where these numbers come from

  • Every value is output from impact-flight.js · solveFlight, re-derived by outputs/agent-scan/gen-face-error-cost-by-club.mjs: twenty solver calls, one baseline and four face angles per club. Nothing here is transcribed or estimated.
  • The mechanism is that offline distance is carry × sin(start direction) + curve, and both terms grow with face angle. A driver carries 261 yd instead of 124, its face steers a larger share of start direction (0.83 against the wedge's 0.68), and it spins 2485 rpm instead of 9000, so the spin axis tilts further for the same face-to-path gap. Long and low-spinning is the same thing as unforgiving.
  • Linear? is the check: 4.00× would be perfectly linear; the observed spread 3.90–4.02× is how far reality bends, which is not far.
  • Carry barely moves — the driver loses 0.8 yd across the full 4°, from 261.3 to 260.5. The whole cost is sideways.
  • The wedge row runs into the engine's 9000 rpm backspin ceiling (raw 9056–9072 rpm), so its curve term — and therefore its offline values — is marginally conservative. Flagged, not hidden.
  • All four rows carry the engine's aero-extrapolated flag. That is the normal state across roughly 94% of the parameter domain, not a fault in these rows.
