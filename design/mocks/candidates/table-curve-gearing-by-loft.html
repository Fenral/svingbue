Curve gearing

One mistake, five clubs.

Read this table across, not down. Every row holds the face-to-path gap fixed and changes only loft. It is the same mistake made with 5 different clubs, and the curve does not stay the same. Loft gears it down.

At an 8° gap, 12° of loft bends the ball 26.7 yd. The identical 8° gap at 44° of loft bends it 14.6 yd: 45.3% less curve from the same error. The mechanism is spin axis. Across that row it falls from 27.7° to 7.8°, a 71.8% drop, while the gap never moves. Loft adds backspin, backspin dominates the spin vector, and the axis tilts less.

Down a column the relationship is close to linear: at 12° loft, gaps of 2/4/6/8° give 7.6 / 14.7 / 21.2 / 26.7 yd, so doubling the mistake roughly doubles the curve. Across a row it is not linear. It decays, and it decays fastest between 12° and 20° of loft.

What the yard figures are, and are not

Spin axis is pure D-plane geometry. It is unfitted and it is the number to trust. Curve in yards is fitted. The raw RK4 Magnus bend is scaled per shot by fitted carry ÷ RK4 carry, which is the projection-scale row above, running 1.14–1.20× at 12° loft down to 0.98–0.99× at 44°. Because the scale falls with loft, the fit amplifies the contrast the table is about. Unfitted, the 8°-gap row runs 22.2 yd to 14.8 yd, a 33.3% drop rather than 45.3%. The direction of the pattern is real in both. The size of it is not.

Not a bag

Club speed is held at 95 mph for every loft, to isolate loft. A real player swings a 44° wedge roughly 20 mph slower than a driver, so the carry row is not a distance chart and these are not your yardages. Only the curve and spin-axis contrast is the point.
